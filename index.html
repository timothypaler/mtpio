July 24 01 Biochemistry and Medicine

I learned that Biochemistry gives medicine its molecular foundation. I initially struggled to connect chemical processes with actual disease, but the discussion helped me see that cellular changes can explain symptoms, guide diagnosis, and influence treatment. Understanding the science beneath a condition will help me care for patients with greater purpose.

Prelim chapter 02 Water and pH

I learned how water, buffers, and pH protect the body's internal balance. The calculations and acid-base relationships were difficult for me at first, so I had to review them step by step. This chapter showed me that even a small change in pH can have major clinical consequences.

Prelim chapter 03 Amino Acids and Peptides

I learned that the properties of amino acids determine how proteins behave. I struggled with the many structures and classifications, but grouping them by charge, polarity, and side-chain behavior made the information more understandable. Repetition helped me move from memorizing names to recognizing patterns.

Prelim chapter 04 Proteins: Determination of Primary Structure

I learned that the exact amino acid sequence forms the basis of every protein's identity and function. I found sequencing methods difficult because each step had to be understood in the correct order. Working through the process carefully taught me that precision at the molecular level matters.

Prelim chapter 05 Proteins: Higher Orders of Structure

I learned how proteins fold into secondary, tertiary, and quaternary structures that make their functions possible. Distinguishing the interactions at each level was challenging, but visualizing the shapes helped me understand why a change in folding can also change health.

Prelim chapter 06 Myoglobin and Hemoglobin

I learned how structure allows myoglobin to store oxygen and hemoglobin to transport it. The binding curves and factors that shift oxygen affinity were difficult for me to interpret, but revisiting them helped me connect molecular behavior with the body's changing oxygen needs.

Prelim chapter 07 Enzymes: Mechanism of Action

I learned how enzymes lower activation energy and allow essential reactions to proceed efficiently. I struggled to follow the sequence of binding, transition-state stabilization, and product release, but breaking the mechanism into stages made the process clearer.

Prelim chapter 08 Enzymes: Kinetics

I learned how Km and Vmax describe enzyme behavior. This was one of the most challenging chapters because I had to understand equations, graphs, and their meaning at the same time. Practice helped me see the graph as a story of how an enzyme responds to changing substrate levels.

Prelim chapter 09 Enzymes: Regulation of Activities

I learned that the body regulates enzymes to coordinate metabolism and maintain balance. The different regulatory mechanisms were easy to mix up, so I focused on the purpose of each one. This chapter reminded me that mastery takes patience, repetition, and the willingness to ask why.

From concept to clinical thinking

Three moments that made it real.

01CASE DISCUSSION

Reflection on SGD: "The Silence Inside the Vehicle" (Carbon Monoxide Poisoning)

+

The SGD "The Silence Inside the Vehicle" was a real eye-opener for me as a first-year medical student. It perfectly bridged our basic biochemistry lectures on hemoglobin with a real-life clinical emergency. Learning how carbon monoxide binds to hemoglobin with over 200 times the affinity of oxygen forming carboxyhemoglobin and shifting the dissociation curve to the left made the molecular concepts finally click. It is terrifying to realize how a silent, odorless gas can so easily hijack the body’s entire oxygen transport system and starve peripheral tissues.

Beyond just memorizing textbook mechanisms, this case highlighted the heavy responsibility we are preparing for. Seeing how a simple competitive inhibition at the molecular level rapidly turns into a fatal clinical scenario emphasized exactly why we need to master these foundations. It was a stark reminder that we aren't just studying pathways to pass our biochemistry exams; we are learning the "why" behind life-saving clinical interventions, like administering 100% oxygen to outcompete the carbon monoxide.

02ONLINE REPORTING

Reflection on Online Reporting: Spectrophotometry

+

I prepared extensively for our Spectrophotometry report, spending hours making sure I truly understood the Beer-Lambert Law and its clinical applications. Because I was assigned as one of the reporters, I wanted to do my part well. When the format shifted to an online setup, I was honestly quite relieved. As someone who gets nervous presenting in front of a crowd, the virtual format felt like a safe space where I could just focus on delivering the material I had worked so hard to digest.

Unfortunately, my relief turned into pure panic when my microphone completely malfunctioned right as it was my turn to speak. I felt so defeated watching all my preparation go to waste in real-time. More than the frustration of the technical glitch, I felt incredibly anxious and shy toward my groupmates. I was terrified they would think I faked the microphone issue just to get out of reporting, and I felt a heavy sense of guilt for not being able to contribute my voice during the actual presentation despite all the work I put in behind the scenes.

03SCIENCE COMMUNICATION

G6PD Infographics

+

Creating the G6PD deficiency infographic was a refreshing and challenging exercise in science communication. In our lectures, it’s easy to get lost in the intricate details of the Hexose Monophosphate (HMP) shunt and the exact mechanisms of oxidative stress on red blood cells. However, this activity forced us to step back and translate complex biochemical concepts—like Heinz bodies, bite cells, and triggers like fava beans or certain medications—into a visually appealing and easily digestible format. It made me realize that you truly have to master the core pathway before you can simplify it for someone else.

Working with my groupmates made the process much more engaging, as we brainstormed how to balance accurate medical information with a clean, uncrowded design. As a first-year medical student, this was a great reminder that knowing the science is only half the battle; the other half is effectively communicating it. This infographic is exactly the kind of tool we will eventually use in the clinics to explain a diagnosis to a patient or a worried parent. It was a creative break from our heavy textbook reading that ultimately taught me a valuable lesson in empathy and patient education.
